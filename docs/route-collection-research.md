# All-country route collection — research foundation

Research started: 2026-09-03  
Countries: Namibia, South Africa, Botswana, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Malawi, Lesotho, Eswatini  
Status: Batches 1 and 2 implemented as 19 curated routes; Batch 3 remains research-only pending specialist validation

## Implementation status

- **Batch 1:** 10 routes implemented in Route Explorer with map coordinates, editable day plans, traveller guidance and one-click My Safari creation.
- **Batch 2:** 9 deeper-travel routes implemented with the same planning fields, shareable route URLs and route-specific search metadata.
- **Logistics layer:** initial per-leg road distance and driving-time estimates implemented for all 19 routes. Low-confidence remote legs are deliberately withheld and marked for local validation; printable route summaries are available.
- **All-route logistics review:** all 19 routes now include dated, named fuel/supply anchors, overnight anchors, gate or permit checks, and links to the official material used for review. Low-confidence remote legs remain deliberately withheld unless an official distance and time are available.
- **My Safari hand-off:** every destination transition now becomes an editable drive item carrying its usable distance/time estimate, route checks, fuel plan and review date into the traveller's day-by-day itinerary.
- **Batch 3:** not implemented. These expedition concepts remain candidates until road, permit, emergency-support and seasonal access checks are complete.
- Route advice is a planning aid. Safety-sensitive details remain visibly dated and should be rechecked with the linked official source before travel.

## Product standard

Every published route should have the same traveller-facing fields:

- Route name and one-sentence promise
- Countries and ordered stop list
- Suggested duration and ideal traveller type
- Best season, with a reason rather than a generic month range
- Vehicle class: standard car, high-clearance SUV, 4x4, or guided/fly-in
- Day-by-day route outline
- Top reasons to choose the route
- Road surface, fuel, border, permit and booking constraints
- Parks, stays, activities and community experiences that can be added to My Safari
- Map coordinates, leg distances and indicative driving times
- Official sources, last-reviewed date and an explicit planning disclaimer

Route information must be written originally. Official route names may be retained when they are established destination brands, with attribution and links.

## Research status

- **Green** — strong official-source support; suitable for the first mapping and validation pass.
- **Amber** — sound route concept, but requires current road, seasonal access, permit or local-operator confirmation.
- **Restricted** — inspiration only; do not promote as an independent route while official advisories warn against travel.

## 1. Namibia

Primary sources: [Visit Namibia routes](https://visitnamibia.com.na/), [Arid Eden](https://visitnamibia.com.na/arid-eden-route-2/), [Four Rivers](https://visitnamibia.com.na/the-four-river-route/), [Omulunga Palm](https://visitnamibia.com.na/the-omulunga-palm-route/), [NWR](https://www.nwr.com.na/)

### Green candidates

1. **Namibia Essentials — 10–14 days**
   - Stops: Windhoek → Sesriem/Sossusvlei → Swakopmund/Walvis Bay → Spitzkoppe → Twyfelfontein/Palmwag → Etosha → Windhoek
   - Best for: first visits, landscapes and wildlife
   - Vehicle: high-clearance SUV; 4x4 preferred for side trips
   - Season: May–October for cooler driving and concentrated wildlife

2. **Arid Northwest and Etosha — 8–12 days**
   - Stops: Swakopmund → Spitzkoppe → Brandberg → Twyfelfontein → Palmwag → Sesfontein → Opuwo/Epupa → western Etosha
   - Best for: experienced self-drivers, geology, conservancies and culture
   - Vehicle: 4x4; remote-road preparation required
   - Research note: combine the official Arid Eden sub-routes into a traveller-friendly circuit without implying all tracks are suitable year-round

3. **Four Rivers Wetlands — 8–12 days**
   - Stops: Rundu → Divundu/Popa Falls → Bwabwata → Kongola → Mudumu/Nkasa Rupara → Katima Mulilo → Ngoma
   - Best for: birding, river wildlife, community conservancies and cross-border extensions
   - Vehicle: high-clearance SUV; 4x4 for selected park tracks
   - Season: dry season for easier park access; wet/green season for birding with access caveats

4. **Omulunga Cultural North — 5–8 days**
   - Stops: Ruacana → Outapi → Oshakati/Ongwediva → Ondangwa/Olukonda → Eenhana → Rundu
   - Best for: living culture, history, markets and a north-to-northeast connector
   - Vehicle: standard car for the core route; SUV for detours
   - Research note: community consultation and respectful photography guidance are essential

5. **Southern Canyon and Atlantic Coast — 8–11 days**
   - Stops: Windhoek → Kalahari/Mariental → Keetmanshoop → Fish River Canyon/Ai-Ais → Lüderitz/Kolmanskop → Aus → NamibRand/Windhoek
   - Best for: geology, desert landscapes, history and slower road trips
   - Vehicle: standard car on core roads; high-clearance SUV preferred
   - Season: April–October; canyon hiking has separate seasonal and medical requirements

## 2. South Africa

Primary sources: [South African Tourism](https://www.southafrica.net/), [Panorama Route](https://www.southafrica.net/za/en/travel/article/road-tripping-along-the-panorama-route), [two-week road trip](https://www.southafrica.net/gl/en/travel/article/two-week-south-african-road-trip-itinerary), [SANParks](https://www.sanparks.org/)

### Green candidates

1. **Cape Town and Garden Route — 8–12 days**
   - Stops: Cape Town → Winelands → Cape Agulhas → Oudtshoorn → Wilderness/Knysna → Plettenberg Bay → Tsitsikamma → Gqeberha
   - Best for: first visits, families, coast, food and mixed activities
   - Vehicle: standard car
   - Season: year-round; September–April for warmer coastal conditions

2. **Panorama and Kruger — 6–9 days**
   - Stops: Johannesburg → Dullstroom/Lydenburg → Graskop → Blyde River Canyon → Hazyview → Kruger National Park
   - Best for: scenery plus Big Five wildlife
   - Vehicle: standard car or SUV
   - Research note: separate realistic Panorama sightseeing time from Kruger gate and camp deadlines

3. **Drakensberg, Battlefields and Zululand — 8–12 days**
   - Stops: Johannesburg or Durban → northern/central Drakensberg → Battlefields → Hluhluwe-iMfolozi → iSimangaliso/St Lucia → Durban
   - Best for: hiking, history, wetlands and wildlife
   - Vehicle: standard car; SUV useful for rural access
   - Season: April–September for hiking; summer for lush landscapes with storms

4. **Wild Coast Journey — 8–12 days**
   - Stops: Gqeberha → Hogsback → Morgan Bay → Coffee Bay/Hole in the Wall → Port St Johns → southern KwaZulu-Natal/Durban
   - Best for: slow travel, beaches, hiking and community stays
   - Vehicle: high-clearance SUV recommended
   - Status: Amber until rural-road, transfer and community-host validation is complete

5. **Kalahari, Falls and Wildflowers — 9–14 days**
   - Stops: Upington → Augrabies Falls → Kgalagadi → Springbok/Namaqua → West Coast
   - Best for: arid landscapes, predators, photography and seasonal flowers
   - Vehicle: high-clearance SUV; 4x4 for specified Kgalagadi routes
   - Season: August–September for flowers, otherwise cooler months for desert travel

## 3. Botswana

Primary sources: [Botswana Tourism Organisation](https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/), [self-drive camping guidance](https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/index.php/travel-info/self-drive-camping), [Okavango Delta](https://botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/okavango-delta), [Moremi](https://botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/moremi-game-reserve), [Kgalagadi](https://www.botswanatourism.co.bw/explore/kgalagadi-transfrontier-park)

### Green candidates

1. **Okavango to Chobe Expedition — 9–14 days**
   - Stops: Maun → Moremi → Khwai → Savuti → Chobe Riverfront → Kasane
   - Best for: experienced safari self-drivers and wildlife-focused trips
   - Vehicle: fully equipped 4x4
   - Constraints: camps and park access should be booked well ahead; deep sand and water crossings vary seasonally

2. **Salt Pans and Chobe — 7–10 days**
   - Stops: Maun → Nxai Pan → Makgadikgadi/Boteti → Nata Sanctuary → Kasane/Chobe
   - Best for: open landscapes, migration, flamingos and a less technical circuit
   - Vehicle: 4x4 for park sectors; high-clearance SUV for a lodge-based version
   - Season: route character changes substantially between dry months and summer rains

3. **Central Kalahari Wilderness — 7–10 days**
   - Stops: Gaborone or Maun → Rakops/Matswere → Deception Valley → Piper Pans → Sunday Pan → exit
   - Best for: remote camping and experienced expedition travellers
   - Vehicle: fully equipped 4x4; conservative fuel and water planning
   - Status: Amber pending current gate, campsite, convoy and track validation

4. **Okavango Panhandle and Tsodilo — 6–9 days**
   - Stops: Maun → Guma/Shakawe/Panhandle → Tsodilo Hills → optional Gcwihaba area → Maun
   - Best for: birding, boating, rock art and quieter travel
   - Vehicle: SUV for the main route; 4x4 for remote detours
   - Research note: include cultural-site etiquette and guide requirements

5. **Southern Botswana and Kgalagadi — 7–11 days**
   - Stops: Gaborone → Khama Rhino Sanctuary → Tswapong/Lepokole → Kgalagadi/Mabuasehube → exit to South Africa or return
   - Best for: overlanders, archaeology, desert wildlife and cross-border extensions
   - Vehicle: 4x4 for Kgalagadi sectors
   - Constraints: some wilderness tracks require more than one vehicle; validate rules route by route

## 4. Zambia

Primary sources: [Zambia Tourism](https://www.zambiatourism.com/), [official self-drive circuits](https://www.zambiatourism.com/self-drive/), [destinations](https://www.zambiatourism.com/destinations/), [waterfalls](https://www.zambiatourism.com/destinations/waterfalls/), [travel and border information](https://www.zambiatourism.com/travel-info/travel-to-zambia/)

### Green candidates

1. **Eastern Safari Route — 8–12 days**
   - Stops: Lusaka → Chipata → South Luangwa/Mfuwe → optional Luambe → return or Malawi extension
   - Best for: walking safaris, wildlife and a Malawi combination
   - Vehicle: SUV for core access; 4x4 for remote sectors and rainy-season travel
   - Season: June–October for wildlife and road reliability

2. **Southern Zambezi Journey — 8–12 days**
   - Stops: Lusaka → Lower Zambezi/Chirundu → Siavonga/Lake Kariba → Livingstone/Victoria Falls
   - Best for: river activities, canoeing, lakes and Victoria Falls
   - Vehicle: SUV; some park access is better by transfer, boat or charter
   - Research note: water-based activity availability depends on season and operator safety rules

3. **Northern Waterfalls Circuit — 16–24 days**
   - Stops: Lusaka → Kundalila → Kasanka → Bangweulu → Lumangwe/Kabwelume → Mporokoso → Kalambo Falls/Mbala → Lake Tanganyika
   - Best for: expedition travel, waterfalls, birding and remote Zambia
   - Vehicle: 4x4 with recovery, fuel and water preparation
   - Status: Amber; official tourism guidance describes this as a multi-week remote self-drive

4. **Kafue and Busanga Plains — 7–11 days**
   - Stops: Lusaka → southern Kafue → central Kafue → Busanga Plains → return
   - Best for: lower-density safari and wilderness
   - Vehicle: high-clearance 4x4; lodge-transfer alternative
   - Season: access is strongly seasonal, especially in the north

5. **Western Plains and Liuwa — 10–15 days**
   - Stops: Lusaka → Kafue → Mongu/Barotse Floodplain → Kalabo → Liuwa Plain → return
   - Best for: remote landscapes, culture, birding and seasonal wildlife
   - Vehicle: 4x4; local logistics support recommended
   - Status: Amber pending floodplain access, ferry/bridge and park-transfer validation

5. **Southern Lowveld and Gonarezhou — 8–12 days**
   - Stops: Great Zimbabwe/Masvingo → Gonarezhou → optional Save Valley/Chimanimani → Mutare or Harare

## Cross-cutting safety and quality rules

1. No route is published until every leg has coordinates, a realistic driving duration, road class and fuel plan.
2. Routes that touch a border must reference the existing Savanna Explorer border record rather than duplicating hours and fees.
3. Park gates, campsite permits, ferry schedules and seasonal closures need a visible last-reviewed date.
4. User-generated and AI-generated routes must inherit route warnings; AI must not bypass Restricted status.
5. Do not recommend night driving on remote or wildlife-heavy roads.
6. Avoid giving a generic “standard car” label when the final lodge, campsite or trailhead requires high clearance.
7. Community and cultural experiences need consent, etiquette and benefit information, not exoticising language.
8. Maps are planning aids, not turn-by-turn navigation. Every route needs an offline list alternative.
